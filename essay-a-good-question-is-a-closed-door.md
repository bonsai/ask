# A good question is a closed door

Ask badly and the other person goes quiet. "He isn't really answering." Almost everyone reaches for the same conclusion: the asker is bad at this.

The conclusion is backwards. **The question was too good.**

## What a good question is

A good question has its answer already prepared.

> Who is the target reader for this book?

That question is flawless. And the answer comes out the way finished writing comes out — organised, quotable, ready to paste into a document. The part that does *not* come out is the inconvenient part. The ambiguity you were privately carrying gets tidied away in the act of answering, and you never find out it was there.

## What a bad question is

A bad question has no answer prepared.

> Is that really true?
> Who does this land with?

There is no through-line here. So the mouth opens. Not because you have decided to speak, but because there is no tidy answer available. **Not being able to answer is not refusal. It is the absence of a shaped answer.**

And the place where no shaped answer exists is exactly the place where you do not have anything yet.

## Why the good question fails

Because **a good question has a slot shaped like the answer.**

Sit down to answer it and you begin arranging. You choose the noun, you drop the caveat, you pick the cleaner example. Arranging takes two minutes. Arranging is the real work, and it is work you already know how to do. So you do it instead of thinking, and the thinking never happens.

A broken question has no slot. Arranging is impossible. Nothing collapses, nothing is concluded, and you sit there at 38 degrees holding something you cannot yet name.

## Three ways to break the form

**Ask for a number.** "Who is the target?" becomes "How many are they?" If the number doesn't exist, you have just discovered that you were writing a plan without one.

**Ask for now.** "What is the strongest category?" becomes "Which one is selling right now?" The abstract is pinned to a moment, and a moment is falsifiable.

**Invert it.** "What is the advantage?" becomes "Why would someone not read this?" A negated question cannot be answered by restating the plan. It demands a reason from outside the plan.

None of these is the question you wanted answered. Each one demands something the tidy version never required. **The question you wanted was the one with no answer — which means the only person who can produce it is you, badly, out loud.**

## The one move

Take your own best question and break its form.

Not a cleverer question. The one you were about to use, the one that felt right. Then notice what it was standing on.

"Who is the target?" was standing on the assumption that a target is a kind of person. "How many?" removes that. "What do they get?" removes another. You did not add insight. You **removed the floor you were standing on**, and you only noticed because the question stopped working.

> The premise left the room. A premise that has left the room cannot quietly disappear again.

**That noticing is the entire product.** Whether a document comes out the other end is irrelevant. A good interview produces a proposal; a broken question produces the thing the proposal was standing on.

## What this means for asking

The instinct is to sharpen the question. That instinct is the problem. Sharpening closes the door.

```
well-formed  答えが既に用意されている   →  two minutes of arranging
broken       受け答えの型がない        →  nothing to arrange, so you think
```

So the skill is not to ask better questions. It is to **break your own, on purpose, and keep the temperature up while the other person gropes.**

The reason they keep talking is that you never gave them anything tidy to hold. Which is uncomfortable, and is the entire mechanism.

## One warning about protocols

Detection is not free.

Step 4 of any questioning protocol — locate the contradiction, name the assumption, surface the gap — is a **15-degree** operation. Contradiction is a judgement. If the detector runs to completion, the temperature has already dropped before the first question is asked, and the session is now an interview rather than a wallbounce.

**Do not detect first. Ask first. Detect afterwards, on paper, alone.**

The order is not stylistic. The order is the method.
